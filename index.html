The idea

One shared machine. Plenty of mischief.

Compiled turns a tiny computer into a tactical playground. The board is a loop of memory slots, the cards are bite-sized commands, and the CPU has four shared registers waiting to be nudged, scrambled, or spectacularly misunderstood.

Your cards and objective stay secret while the board and CPU stay public. Plant one instruction, hit Run, and see whether your clever plan survives contact with everyone else's plans. You win when both parts of your objective are true at the end of your run.

01

Plant your code

Drop commands like MOVE #3, R0, XCHG R2, R0, or BRA -2 into the loop.

02

Let it rip

Watch four-bit values flip, wrap, and surprise you. Empty slots play NOP, which is computer-speak for “nothing to see here.”

03

Hide your scheme

Meet two conditions at once without giving away the game. A suspiciously helpful move is often the most dangerous one.

How a turn feels

Every turn is a tiny plot twist.

  1. Stage

    Choose a card and a spot in the loop. Overwrite an old command if you dare.

  2. Review

    Take one last look at your plan, then commit it and draw a fresh hand.

  3. Advance

    The machine steps forward and starts following the trail you left behind.

  4. Execute

    Commands fire, branches bounce, and the CPU keeps going until the loop tells it to stop.

  5. Check

    At the end, the machine checks your secret objective. Get both conditions right and claim the win.

Know your operands

  • Signed immediate values: -5 to +5, excluding zero. Unsigned immediates: 1 to 15.
  • Binary operations can also read a source register instead of an immediate value.
  • XCHG swaps two registers. REV reverses all four bits.
  • BRA jumps -3 to +3 slots, excluding zero. Branch chains stop at the five-instruction limit.
  • NOP stops a run without changing registers or flags. It can be dealt as a card as well as encountered on empty slots.

The fun part of four bits

Small numbers. Sneaky possibilities.

Read the machine in signed numbers, unsigned numbers, hexadecimal, or binary. The bits stay the same; what they mean can change everything.

Push a value past its limit and watch it wrap around. In Compiled, even a tiny number can start a very large argument.

Z/N

Read the result

Z means the result is zero. N mirrors the top bit: negative when read as signed, or 8–15 when read as unsigned.

C/V

Carry and overflow differ

C tracks unsigned carry or borrow; shifts and rotations copy the outgoing bit into C. V reports signed arithmetic overflow. Unsigned arithmetic clears V.

+1

Try the boundary

On the bits for 7, ADD #1, R0 sets V=1 and C=0. ADDU #1, R0 produces the same bits with both flags clear.
